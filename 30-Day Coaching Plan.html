Week 1 · Apr 28 – May 4

Baseline and Framework Build

0%
complete
Actions 0 / 5
  • Audit all Stage 2+ opportunities: identify which have a documented business problem and stakeholder owner, and which don't.
  • Draft a one-page value narrative template with four fields: use case, business problem, stakeholder owner, expected outcome. Apply it to your top 3 deals.
  • Learn the MEDDPICC framework (Metrics, Economic Buyer, Decision Criteria, Decision Process, Paper Process, Identify Pain, Champion, Competition). Build a simple scorecard template you'll use on every deal.

  • Review CRM hygiene — ensure deal stages reflect real buyer progression, not optimism.
What Success Looks Like

Top 3 active deals have a written value narrative. Your discovery checklist exists and has been used on at least one call. You can name which deals have genuine urgency and which are at risk — without looking at your CRM.

Manager Coaching Questions

  • Does my value narrative for [Deal X] reflect what the buyer actually said, or what I assumed?
  • Where am I most likely to stall in pipeline progression right now?

Week 2 · May 5 – May 11

MEDDPICC in Practice

0%
complete
Actions 0 / 5
  • Score your top 5 active deals against the full MEDDPICC scorecard. For each dimension, mark it green (confirmed), yellow (partial), or red (unknown). Be honest.
  • For each red or yellow dimension, write one specific question you'll ask to close the gap — then use those questions on your next call with that account.
  • Identify which MEDDPICC dimension is most consistently weak across your pipeline (e.g. Economic Buyer access, Paper Process clarity). That's your personal coaching focus for the week.
  • Bring one scored MEDDPICC deal card to your 1:1. Walk through it dimension by dimension — let your manager challenge your yellows and reds.
  • For one stalled or slow-moving deal, use MEDDPICC to diagnose exactly where it's stuck — then write a specific next action to unblock it.
What Success Looks Like

Every top active deal has a completed MEDDPICC scorecard. You can name the weakest dimension in each deal and have a clear plan to address it. In your 1:1, you can walk through at least one deal card without being caught off guard by a gap your manager spots.

Manager Coaching Questions
  • In [Deal X], do I actually have the Economic Buyer engaged, or am I working through a champion who doesn't have authority?
  • Which MEDDPICC dimension am I most likely to gloss over, and why?
  • Looking at my reds and yellows — which deals should I slow down on or disqualify?

Week 3 · May 12 – May 18

Executive Framing

0%
complete
Actions 0 / 5
  • Write an executive summary for your top 2 strategic deals. Format: 3–5 sentences. Lead with business impact. End with a clear recommendation. No jargon.
  • Share one executive summary with your manager before your 1:1. Use the meeting to workshop the framing — what's sharp, what's still too operational?
  • Rewrite one existing deal recap to lead with the outcome and recommendation, not the activity log.
  • Practice a 60-second verbal summary of one deal as if briefing a VP. Record yourself or do it live with your manager.
  • Identify one deal where you've been reacting to the buyer's process. Write what you'd do differently to reestablish control and set the next milestone.
What Success Looks Like

You have a written executive summary for every strategic deal. You can deliver a 60-second verbal deal summary that leads with business impact — not features, not call history. Your manager can tell you what's sharp and what still sounds too operational.

Manager Coaching Questions
  • Does my executive summary for [Deal X] sound like something I'd send to a VP buyer?
  • Where am I leading with activity instead of impact?
  • Which deal do you think I have the weakest point of view on right now?

30-Day Success Definition

At the end of 30 days, you will have demonstrated consistent deal execution across three dimensions. These are not subjective — each is verifiable in a pipeline review or 1:1.

  • 01
    Business value is documented for 90% of qualified opportunities — in the CRM or deal brief, ready for a deal review or internal handoff. Not in your head.
  • 02
    MEDDPICC is operational — every active deal has a scored card. You can walk into any 1:1, name the red and yellow gaps by dimension, and explain exactly what you're doing to close them.
  • 03
    Executive communication is sharp — you can summarize any strategic deal in 60 seconds, lead with business impact, and offer a clear recommendation.

If you hit those three marks, you've built real operating habits — not a one-time exercise. The discovery checklist and value narrative template you built in Week 1 should be the tools you're still using in Month 3.
